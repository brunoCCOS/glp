\begin{center}
  \textbf{\MakeUppercase{Abstract}}
\end{center}

\noindent
This dissertation presents a categorical roadmap through 
the theory of Graphical Algebra towards a compositional
calculus for Optimization problems. 
It investigates the intrinsic relationship between composition
/variable elimination and that of optimization itself.
Showing how under concrete settings, optimization emerges
from the internal structure of quantale-categories.
The central result of this work is the presentation of a
complete monoidal theory for right-hand side parametric linear programming.

It builts up from the necessary notions of 
quantales and relations and it's extension towards metric-spaces
through the Lawvere-Quantale. Then it recalls and explore the already existing
diagrammatic algebra for polyhedrals \textbf{GPA} providing the full demonstration
of it's soundness and completeness. In the end, it incremments this existing theory
through a elegant and simple approach, adding one single generator and three axioms,
lifting the language from simply expressing polyhedrals to a more powerfull
algebra capable of denoting Parametric Linear Programs.

The diagrammatic calculus developed here represents a step
toward the broader goal of providing a more intuitive and practical language
for computational and mathematical reasoning in optimization.
In this setting, technical details can become difficult to navigate,
particularly when reasoning about complex systems.
String diagrams help by hiding many of these particulars behind
a compact graphical syntax,
thereby making the underlying abstract structure
and core properties more directly accessible to manipulation and reasoning.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent
Keywords: category theory; string diagrams; linear programming; convex
optimization; symmetric monoidal categories; weighted relations; quantales;
graphical algebra; normal forms; completeness.
