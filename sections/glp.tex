\chapter{The Linear Programming Category}\label{sec:glp}

We finally reach the point of most interest. Now introduce the
final and most expressive theory of the thesis:
a  graphical algebra for parametric linear programs.
Linear programs combine  both the inequality structure of polyhedra
and the notion of an objective function.
The central result of this section is the presentation of a
complete monoidal theory for right-hand side parametric linear programming.

\section{Semantics: the prop of linear programs}\label{sec:lprel}

The semantic category of this chapter is called $\mathbf{LPRel}$. Its
morphisms are the value functions of right-hand side parametric linear
programs, regarded as cost-valued relations in
$\mathbf{Rel}(\extendR)$. Here $\extendR$ carries the optimization
quantale introduced in the chapter on composition and order, with
inf-addition $(+\infty)+(-\infty)=+\infty$.

\begin{definition}[LP value function]\label{def:lp-value}
    Let $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$. A function
    $v:\mathbb{R}^m\times\mathbb{R}^n\to\extendR$ is an
    \emph{LP value function of type $m\to n$} if there exist
    $k\in\mathbb{N}$, $A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times k}$,
    $B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}$, $C\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}$,
    $e\in\mathbb{R}^p$ and $c\in\mathbb{R}^k$ such that
    \[
        v(x,y)=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^k}\{c^\top z \mid Az\ge Bx+Cy+e\},
        \qquad \inf\varnothing=+\infty .
    \]
    We call $(A,B,C,e,c)$ a \emph{presentation} of $v$. Different
    presentations may define the same value function.
\end{definition}

By the equivalence proved in Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}, the LP
value functions of type $m\to n$ are exactly the extended piecewise
affine LP functions on $\mathbb{R}^{m+n}$. A constant offset $d$ in the
objective adds no generality: introduce a fresh decision variable
$z_0$, the two constraints $z_0\ge1$ and $-z_0\ge-1$, and the cost
$d\,z_0$. Allowing $k=0$ is a convenience. With no decision variables,
the infimum is taken over the one-point set $\mathbb{R}^0$, so it is
$0$ when the constraints hold and $+\infty$ otherwise.

\begin{definition}[The prop $\mathbf{LPRel}$]\label{def:lprel}
    The prop $\mathbf{LPRel}$ has the natural numbers as objects, with
    $n$ interpreted as $\mathbb{R}^n$. A morphism $m\to n$ is an LP
    value function of type $m\to n$; two programs with the same value
    function are the \emph{same} morphism. Composition, identities,
    tensor and symmetries are those of $\mathbf{Rel}(\extendR)$:
    \begin{align*}
        (w\circ v)(x,u) &= \inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^n}\bigl(v(x,y)+w(y,u)\bigr),\\
        (v\otimes v')\bigl((x,x'),(y,y')\bigr) &= v(x,y)+v'(x',y'),\\
        \mathrm{id}_n(x,y) &=
        \begin{cases}0 & x=y,\\ +\infty & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
    \end{align*}
    where the sums use inf-addition. The symmetry is defined like the
    identity, with the coordinates swapped. Hom-sets carry the quantale order
    \[
        v\sqsubseteq w \iff v(x,y)\ge w(x,y)\ \text{for all }(x,y),
    \]
    that is, a morphism is smaller when it is more costly.
\end{definition}

For $\mathbf{LPRel}$ to be a sub-prop of $\mathbf{Rel}(\extendR)$, the
LP value functions must be closed under these operations. This is the
point where the inf-addition convention matters.

\begin{lemma}[Closure]\label{lem:lprel-closure}
    Identities and symmetries are LP value functions, and LP value
    functions are closed under composition and tensor. Composition and
    tensor are monotone with respect to $\sqsubseteq$. Hence
    $\mathbf{LPRel}$ is an ordered sub-prop of $\mathbf{Rel}(\extendR)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Identities and symmetries are presented with no decision variables,
    by pairs of opposite inequalities. For composition, put the two
    programs side by side, add their costs, and treat the shared
    interface $y$ as an extra decision variable with zero cost. The
    result is again an RHS-parametric program in the outer variables.
    Tensor is the same construction without a shared variable. The only
    point to check is the boundary case: if one stage is infeasible and
    the other unbounded, the merged program is infeasible. This matches
    inf-addition, $(+\infty)+(-\infty)=+\infty$. Monotonicity holds
    because infima and inf-addition are monotone in each argument.
\end{proof}

Polyhedral relations sit inside $\mathbf{LPRel}$ as the programs whose
cost is identically zero. As in the chapter on composition and order, we
identify a relation $P$ with the cost-valued relation that is $0$ on
pairs in $P$ and $+\infty$ elsewhere, and we use the same letter for both.

\begin{proposition}[Polyhedral relations in $\mathbf{LPRel}$]\label{prop:indicator-embedding}
    This identification defines an identity-on-objects strict symmetric
    monoidal functor $\mathbf{PolyRel}\to\mathbf{LPRel}$. The functor is
    faithful, and it is an order embedding: $P\subseteq Q$ if and only if
    $P\sqsubseteq Q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    If $P=\{(x,y)\mid Bx+Cy+e\le 0\}$, then $P$, read as a cost-valued
    relation, is presented with $k=0$ by the constraints $0\ge Bx+Cy+e$.
    It therefore lies in $\mathbf{LPRel}$. Composing in $\mathbf{LPRel}$,
    \[
        \inf_y\bigl(P(x,y)+Q(y,u)\bigr)
    \]
    is $0$ exactly when some $y$ satisfies $(x,y)\in P$ and $(y,u)\in Q$,
    and $+\infty$ otherwise. This is the relational composite
    $Q\circ P$. Tensor, identities and symmetries agree in the same way,
    because $0+0=0$ and $+\infty$ is absorbing. Finally,
    $P(x,y)\ge Q(x,y)$ holds for all $(x,y)$ exactly when every pair in
    $P$ lies in $Q$. This gives faithfulness and the order embedding.
\end{proof}

Observe how composition in $\mathbf{LPRel}$ recalls the composition of
value functions derived in Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}. We now build
it into the syntax, so that diagrams handle the bookkeeping of the
infima for us and we can work with the components directly.

The algebra $\text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$ extends
$\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ with a single new generator
whose semantics encodes the
linear objective function $c^\top x$. This new generator lifts
$\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$
from polyhedral constraints to full linear programs by attaching a
linear cost to the feasible region.

\end{definition}

The axioms represent expected properties of the linear cost function.

\begin{itemize}
    \item (linearity) express the linear property $f(x) + f(y) = f(x+y)$.
    Observe that $f$ is the interpretation of the $\inlinegen{linear}$ and the
    sum denotes the vertical composition; composing two $\inlinegen{linear}$ vertically
    (adding) is the same as adding the wires in a single $\inlinegen{linear}$.

    \item (zeroeq) is the tautological fact that $\inf 0 = 0$

    \item (inf) is the epigraph property, this is, the fact
        that the infimum of the epigraph of a function, is the 
        function itself $g(y)=\inf\{x:(y,x)\in\operatorname{epi}(f)\}=\inf_{x\ge f(y)}x=f(y).$ 
\end{itemize}

From this simple axioms, we get immediatly another core property of linear programs,
namely, that they are invariant to stochastics matrices.

This is a pattern present in every algebra discussed so far, 
where a particular class of matrices is invariant to one of it's core generators.
\footnote{In the digrammatic calculi of Linear subspaces (Graphical Linear Algebra)
one can show that surjective matrices are invariant to $\inlinegen{codiscard}$
which is a very important feature in the completeness of the language itself.
The same occurs in Graphical Polyhedral Algebra, where non-negative matrices slide through the $\inlinegen{ge}$.
Other works have spotted the same pattern, such as 
\cite{stein2024gqa} which shows that a generator representing the $l_2$ norm is 
invariant to orthogonal matrices (also are normal distributed random variables).}
Here, the new class of matrices that interact with the new generator
$\inlinegen{linear}$ is that of stochastic matrices, agreeing with what one
would expect when discussing linear problems.

Graphical Polyhedral Algebra is a theory with inequations, and
Graphical Linear Programming inherits them. Its free prop is therefore
ordered, in the same way as in Definition~\ref{def:free-gpa-conic}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:free-glp}
    We write $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}$ for the ordered prop
    generated by Graphical Linear Programming. Its terms $m\to n$ are
    built from the generators of $\mathbf{GPA}$ and $\inlinegen{linear}$
    by composition and tensor. Its order is the smallest precongruence
    containing the inequations of $\mathbf{GPA}$, both directions of
    every equation (old and new), and the symmetric monoidal structural
    laws. Two terms are equal when the order holds in both directions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interpretation]\label{def:glp-interpretation}
    The interpretation
    $\llbracket{-}\rrbracket:\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}\to\mathbf{LPRel}$
    is the unique strict symmetric monoidal functor that sends each
    generator of $\mathbf{GPA}$ to its polyhedral relation, read as a
    cost-valued relation through
    Proposition~\ref{prop:indicator-embedding}, and sends the cost
    generator to $\llbracket\inlinegen{linear}\rrbracket(\bullet,t)=t$.
    The cost generator lies in $\mathbf{LPRel}$: it is presented by
    $\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\{z\mid z\ge t,\ -z\ge -t\}$.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:lprel-closure} ensures that composites of generators
    stay in $\mathbf{LPRel}$.
\end{definition}

The following corollary reveals the geometric meaning of the normal form:
the $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ part of every $\text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$ diagram encodes the
epigraph of the corresponding value function. This is the key structural
insight connecting the syntax to the semantic equivalence of
Proposition~\ref{prop:equalepi}.

\begin{corollary}[Epigraph Form]\label{cor:epigraph form}
    Given a diagram $d \in \text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$ in normal form, it can always
    be rewritten as a diagram $d'$ of the form:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \input{tikz/EpigraphFormGLP.tex}
    \end{figure}
    where the semantics of the dotted diagram is
    \[\left\{(t,y)\mid \exists\, x,\; A'x + a \ge [B\; -C]\, y,\; t\ge x,\; y =
    \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \end{bmatrix}\right\}\].
    This is the epigraph of the original diagram $d$
    \begin{align*}
        [d](y_1, y_2) &= \inf_{z} \; z \quad \text{s.t. } A z + a \ge B y_1 - C y_2 \\
        \mathrm{epi}([d]) &= \left\{(t, y_1, y_2) \mid \exists\, z \le t,\;
            A z + a \ge B y_1 - C y_2 \right\}
    \end{align*}
    such that $A = [A' \;C]$. We call this the \emph{epigraph form} of a linear program.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Starting from $d$ in normal form, split the relation generator along
    $A = [A' \; C]$ and pull the resulting $C$-block through the $\inlinegen{ge}$
    generator using the antipode axiom:
    \input{tikz/EpigraphProof.tex}
    The resulting diagram is precisely the claimed form $d'$, with the
    dotted box exhibiting $\mathrm{epi}([d])$ as a $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ diagram.
\end{proof}

\section{Presentation results for \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{LPRel}$}{LPRel}}

With the syntax and its normal form in place, we are now ready to prove
the three presentation properties for $\text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$.

\begin{lemma}[Expressivity]
    For every $f : m \to n$ in $\mathbf{LPRel}$ there exists a
    $\Sigma$-term $s : m \to n$ in
    $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}}(m,n)$
    such that $[s] = f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof} 
    Any RHS Parametric linear program can be written as 
    $\inf_z \{ c^\top z \mid Ax + b \ge By$ therefore is enough to show that we
    can always depict such a problem. The following diagram provides the required
    syntactic representative:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \input{tikz/ProofExpressivity.tex}
    \end{figure}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness]
   If $s = t$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}$, then $[s] = [t]$
   in $\mathbf{LPRel}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    All axioms inherited from $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ are already sound by the
    results of the previous section. It suffices to verify that the new
    axioms involving the LP generator preserve the semantics, which
    follows by direct computation:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \input{tikz/ProofSoundness.tex}
    \end{figure}
\end{proof}

The final result of the thesis is the completeness theorem for
$\text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$. The proof brings together all the main tools developed
throughout: the epigraph form of Corollary~\ref{cor:epigraph form}, the
shared epigraph property of Proposition~\ref{prop:equalepi}, the
completeness of $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$, and the FME-based normal form.

\begin{lemma}[Completeness]
   If $[c] = [d]$ in $\mathbf{LPRel}$, then $c = d$ in
   $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Let $v_1 = [c]$ and $v_2 = [d]$. By assumption $v_1 = v_2$, so
    Proposition~\ref{prop:equalepi} gives
    $\mathrm{epi}(v_1) = \mathrm{epi}(v_2) =: E$.

    By Corollary~\ref{cor:epigraph form}, both $c$ and $d$ admit
    epigraph forms $c = \inlinegen{linear} ; c^*$ and $d = \inlinegen{linear} ; d^*$
    with $[c^*] = [d^*] = E$ in $\mathbf{PolyRel}$. Since $c^*$ and $d^*$
    are $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ diagrams with the same denotation, completeness of
    $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ gives $c^* = d^*$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$;
    in particular both reduce to the same $\text{\bfseries Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}$ normal form.
    Composing this common diagram with the leftover $\inlinegen{linear}$ collapses
    $c$ and $d$ to the same $\text{\bfseries Graphical Linear Programming}$ diagram:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \input{tikz/ProofCompletenessLemma.tex}
    \end{figure}
    Therefore $c = \inlinegen{linear} ; c^* = \inlinegen{linear} ; d^* = d$.
\end{proof}

As a consequence of the three lemmas above:

\begin{theorem}
    $\mathbf{LPRel}$ is presented by $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GLP}}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    By the preceding three lemmas.
\end{proof}

Finally, we've built, through the addition of one generator and three axioms,
an elegant and complete diagrammatic theory for parametric linear programming.
This result does unlock a new algebraic and computational way to
interact with LP problems. A linear
program is ordinarily presented as a function to be evaluated, an
$x \mapsto c^\top x$ subject to constraints, solved from the outside by an
algorithm. Here it is instead a morphism, an object with an interface that
composes with others of its kind, and solving it, and
eliminating one of its variables are all the same operation, composition in
$\mathbf{LPRel}$.

\section{The complete transportation program}\label{sec:glp-travel}

We return to the constraint diagram of
Section~\ref{sec:gpa-travel} and add the monetary costs $1,2,4$ on the
roads $A\to B$, $B\to C$, and $A\to C$. The constraints stay exactly as
they were; only the objective is new.

The objective is attached road by road. For a price $c$, the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[axpic]
    \pic (g1) at (0.500,0.000) {linear};
    \pic[val=c] (g2) at (1.500,0.000) {coscalar};
    \draw (g1-out-0) -- (g2-in-0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
denotes the cost $(\bullet,x)\mapsto cx$: the linear generator charges
its wire value $t$, and the reflected scalar imposes $t=cx$. We call it
a \emph{price tag}. A black node joins the price tag to the wire carrying
the flow, so the flow is charged at price $c$ without being changed.
This is the same construction used for $c^\top x$ in the expressivity
proof above.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{tikz/TravelProgram}}
    \caption{The complete transportation program in Graphical Linear
    Programming. It is the constraint diagram of
    Figure~\ref{fig:travel-constraints} with one price tag on each road.
    The dotted boxes group the stage programs $U:2\to1$ and $W:2\to0$.}
    \label{fig:travel-program}
\end{figure}

Compared with Figure~\ref{fig:travel-constraints}, three price tags have
been added: price $1$ on $x_{AB}$ and price $4$ on $x_{AC}$ inside the
first stage, and price $2$ on $x_{BC}$ inside the second. All other nodes
are polyhedral generators, which denote indicator costs: $0$ when their
constraint holds and $+\infty$ otherwise.

Composition in $\mathbf{LPRel}$ adds costs and minimizes over internal
wires. Inside each box this gives the two stage programs of
Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
U(p,q,f) &= \inf_{x_{AB},x_{AC}\ge0}
\{x_{AB}+4x_{AC}\mid x_{AB}+x_{AC}=p,\ x_{AB}=q,\ p\le f\},\\
W(q,g) &= \inf_{x_{BC}\ge0}\{2x_{BC}\mid x_{BC}=q,\ x_{BC}\le g\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Composing the boxes through $q$, and the fuel split $F=f+g$, eliminates the
internal wires $f$, $g$, and $q$. The whole diagram has interface
$(p,F)\to *$ and denotes
\[
\inf_{q,f}\bigl(U(p,q,f)+W(q,F-f)\bigr)=V(p,F),
\]
which is the formula of Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}. No infimum was
written by hand: each one comes from an internal wire. Thus the same
connections that eliminate feasibility variables in Graphical Polyhedral
Algebra also minimize costs in Graphical Linear Programming.

The values can be read off the diagram. For $p=F=1$, the split forces
$f=1$ and $g=0$, so $q=0$ and all traffic takes the direct road:
$V(1,1)=4$. For $p=1$, $F=2$, the choice $f=g=1$ and $q=1$ sends all traffic
through $B$: $V(1,2)=1+2=3$. For $p\ge0$ and $F<p$, no split satisfies
$p\le f$ and $q\le g$ with $q\ge0$, so $V(p,F)=+\infty$.

Finally, the three price tags can be merged into one linear generator.
By (linearity), two linear generators in parallel equal a single linear
generator followed by a white comultiplication; applying it twice gives a
single generator whose value is split among the three prices. This is the
first step towards the normal form of Theorem~\ref{thm:NormalFormGLP}.
