\chapter{Polyhedral Relations}\label{sec:gpa}

We now turn to a combinatorially richer class of objects: polyhedra and polyhedral
cones. These are the fundamental geometric structures underlying linear
programming, and they admit a categorical treatment entirely analogous to
the one developed for linear and affine relations.
As before, composition is governed by polyhedral projection,
which is precisely variable elimination, and it is this
operation that categorical composition will simulate diagrammatically.

The morphisms encode feasibility constraints through linear inequalities.
Through the quantale framework, these constraints have indicator costs $0$ and
$+\infty$. Their diagrammatic axiomatization is a Symmetric Monoidal
Inequality Theory (SMIT)~\cite{bonchi2021polyhedral}. This theory provides the
structural foundation for representing linear programs, where feasibility
constraints are combined with a linear objective.

In what follows we present the semantics from polyhedral cones to general
polyhedra, then construct the corresponding graphical algebras in the same
order. The normal forms and comparison results are stated as facts of the
existing theory; only soundness, expressivity, and completeness are proved here.

Recall how we moved from Graphical Linear Algebra to Graphical Affine Algebra: we added
one generator that allowed us to write constants. Here we will add a
generator that lets us write inequalities. If we start without the affine
point, we get polyhedral cones. If we include it, we get general polyhedra.
The four categories fit this simple distinction:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{without constants} & \text{with constants}\\
\hline
\text{equations} & \mathbf{LinRel} & \mathbf{AffRel}\\
\text{inequalities} & \mathbf{PCRel} & \mathbf{PolyRel}
\end{array}
\]
We do not lose equations when we add inequalities: $u=v$ can be written
as $u\ge v$ and $v\ge u$. Nor do we change how relations compose.
We simply gain more ways to describe them.

\section{Semantics: from polyhedral cones to polyhedra}\label{sec:gpa-semantics}

We begin by recalling the standard definitions of polyhedral cones and the corresponding semantic category.

\begin{definition}\label{def:pcform}
    A set $C \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is called a \emph{polyhedral cone} if
    there exists a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ such that
    \[
        C = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid Ax \ge 0\}.
    \]
\end{definition}

We use polyhedral cones as morphisms to expose their compositional and
monoidal structure. A cone in a product space defines a binary relation and
can equivalently be represented by its indicator function.

\begin{definition}[The prop $\mathbf{PCRel}$ of polyhedral cones]
    Let $k$ be an ordered field, in this case the real numbers. The prop
    $\mathbf{PCRel}_k$ is defined as follows:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Objects are natural numbers $n \in \mathbb{N}$, interpreted
            as the space $k^n$.
        \item A morphism $n \to m$ is a polyhedral cone
            $R \subseteq k^n \times k^m$
            for which there exist $p \in \mathbb{N}$ and
            $A \in k^{p \times (n+m)}$ such that
            \[
                R = \left\{ (x,y) \in k^n \times k^m \;\middle|\;
                A \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \ge 0 \right\}.
            \]
        \item Composition is relational composition.
        \item The monoidal product on objects is addition $n \otimes m := n+m$,
            and on morphisms is the Cartesian product.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

A polyhedral cone relation can be described by pairs $(x,y)$ satisfying
$Ax\ge By$ for suitable matrices $A$ and $B$. Composition is relational
composition. Polyhedral cones are closed under this operation and under
Cartesian product, so they can be combined sequentially or in parallel.

Notice the connection with the previous chapter: where we could write
$Ax=By$, we can now write $Ax\ge By$. We still have no constants, and
setting all variables to zero still satisfies the constraints. This is
why Graphical Linear Algebra is our starting point for the language of cones.
Adding the inequality generator will let us describe exactly the
relations in $\mathbf{PCRel}$.

The same construction extends from polyhedral cones to general polyhedra.

\begin{definition}\label{polyform}
    A set $P \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is called a \emph{polyhedron} if there
    exist $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ such that
    \[
        P = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid Ax \ge b\}.
    \]
\end{definition}

A polyhedral cone is the special case $b=0$. General polyhedra admit affine
constraints and may be empty; they need not contain the origin. In that light,
one can define the category of polyhedral relations as:

\begin{definition}[The prop $\mathbf{PolyRel}$ of polyhedra]
    Let $k$ be an ordered field. The prop $\mathbf{PolyRel}_k$ is defined
    as follows:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Objects are natural numbers $n \in \mathbb{N}$, interpreted
            as the space $k^n$.
        \item A morphism $n \to m$ is a polyhedron
            $R \subseteq k^n \times k^m$
            for which there exist $p \in \mathbb{N}$,
            $A \in k^{p \times (n+m)}$, and $b \in k^p$ such that
            \[
                R = \left\{ (x,y) \in k^n \times k^m \;\middle|\;
                A \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + b \ge 0 \right\}.
            \]
        \item Composition is relational composition.
        \item The monoidal product on objects is addition $n \otimes m := n+m$,
            and on morphisms is the Cartesian product.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

As with cone relations, a polyhedral relation may be described directly by
constraints on its input and output values:
$xRy \iff Ax+b\ge By+c$ for suitable matrices $A,B$ and vectors $b,c$.
This is a relational formulation of the same inequality description.


Once again, the affine point makes the difference. Without it we write
$Ax\ge By$; with it we can write $Ax+b\ge By$. We can therefore start
with Graphical Linear Algebra, add inequalities, and then add the affine point.
Or we can start with Graphical Affine Algebra and add inequalities. Including the
axioms for both generators gives the same language of polyhedra.

This is what we mean by the affine version of cones: we allow constants
in the inequalities. We do not mean that every polyhedron is a shifted
cone; a bounded interval is already a counterexample. Cones remain a
special case, so every relation in $\mathbf{PCRel}$ is also a relation
in $\mathbf{PolyRel}$, composed in exactly the same way.

In both categories, relational composition eliminates the shared variables.
Projection and Cartesian product preserve polyhedral cones and polyhedra,
so these operations stay within the stated classes
\cite[Definition~2 and Section~5]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.
The hom-sets are ordered by inclusion of relations.

We construct the diagrammatic language in two stages. First, we extend
Graphical Linear Algebra with inequalities to represent $\mathbf{PCRel}$.
We then add the affine point to represent general polyhedra.

\section{Syntax: from the conic fragment to Graphical Polyhedral Algebra}
\label{sec:gpa-syntax}

We start with the smaller language from Definition~\ref{def:linear-syntax}:
Graphical Linear Algebra, without the affine point. We keep its generators and
equations, and add the inequality generator with its axioms. This is
the ``linear fragment of Graphical Affine Algebra'' mentioned below.

We begin with the linear fragment of the previous chapter and add the
inequality generator $\inlinegen{ge}:1\to1$, interpreted as
$\llbracket \inlinegen{ge} \rrbracket = \{(x,y)\in k^2\mid x\ge y\}$.

\begin{definition}[Graphical Polyhedral Cones Algebra]\label{def:gpa-conic}
    We call $\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}$ the SMIT obtained by extending the
    linear fragment of Graphical Affine Algebra with the inequality generator.
    Its additional equations and inequations are the following:
    \input{tikz/AxiomsGPAConic.tex}
    This fragment axiomatises polyhedral cones, where no affine offset
    is present.
\end{definition}

The linear fragment excludes the $\inlinegen{affine}$ generator and its axioms;
it is precisely Graphical Linear Algebra. As in the previous chapter, we use
the extended theory to generate a PROP.

\begin{definition}[The PROP generated by the conic theory]\label{def:free-gpa-conic}
    We write $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}$ for the ordered
    PROP generated by Definition~\ref{def:gpa-conic}. Its objects are
    natural numbers, and its terms $m\to n$ are diagrams built from the
    linear generators and $\inlinegen{ge}$ by composition and tensor.
    The order is generated by the displayed inequations, the equations
    in both directions, and the symmetric monoidal structural laws,
    closed under composition and tensor. Morphisms are terms identified
    when the order is derivable in both directions.
\end{definition}

An inequation records inclusion of relations; equality means that the
inclusions hold in both directions. Interpreting the generators gives
$\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket:
\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}\to\mathbf{PCRel}$ once the
soundness checks below establish that the interpretation respects these
identifications.

As in Graphical Affine Algebra, every diagram admits a normal form.

\begin{proposition}[Finitely generated normal form]\label{prop:gpa-generated-form}
    After bending its inputs into outputs, every diagram in
    $\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}$ is equal to a diagram $d_V:0\to N$, where
    $N$ is the total number of external wires and
    \[
        [d_V]=\{V\lambda\mid\lambda\in k^r,\ \lambda\ge0\}
    \]
    for some $V\in k^{N\times r}$.

        \centering
        \input{tikz/GPABentNormalForm.tex}
        \caption{Bending the inputs of $s:m\to n$ gives a diagram
        $\widehat{s}:0\to m+n$, with the $x$ coordinates first.
        Its finitely generated normal form $d_V$ generates
        $\lambda\ge0$ and applies $V:k^r\to k^{m+n}$. Each labelled
        wire represents a bundle of coordinates.}
        \label{fig:gpa-bent-normal-form}
    \end{figure}
\end{proposition}

Here $d_V$ is the diagram that generates $r$ non-negative values and
applies the linear map $V$. This is the second normal form: the relation
is described by non-negative combinations of the columns of $V$.
Neither normal form is unique.
The existence of these forms is established in
\cite[Theorems~14 and~18]{bonchi2021polyhedral}; the technical derivations
are collected in Appendix~\ref{appendix:gpa-proofs}.

\begin{lemma}[Comparison of finitely generated diagrams]\label{lem:gpa-comparison}
    Let $V\in k^{N\times r}$ and $W\in k^{N\times q}$.
    If $V=WC$ for a matrix $C\in k^{q\times r}$ with non-negative
    entries, then $d_V\subseteq d_W$ is derivable in
    $\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}$.
\end{lemma}

This is the comparison fact needed for completeness. It states that a
non-negative combination of generating columns gives a diagrammatic
inclusion~\cite[Lemmas~49--50]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.

    If $s=t$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}$, then
    $[s]=[t]$ in $\mathbf{PCRel}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    All axioms except those involving the newly introduced $\ge$
    generator are already present in $\mathbf{GAA}$ and therefore
    verified. It suffices to check that the new axioms preserve the

\end{proof}

The same checks show that the inequations are interpreted as inclusions.

\begin{lemma}[Expressivity]
    For every $f:m\to n$ in $\mathbf{PCRel}$ there exists a term
    $s:m\to n$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}$ such that $[s]=f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    By Definition~\ref{def:pcform}, every polyhedral cone has the form
    $\{x \mid Ax \ge 0,\ x \in \mathbb{R}^n\}$. This is precisely the
    interpretation of the diagram:

        \centering
        \begin{tikzpicture}[axpic]
            \pic (g1) at (0.500,0.500) {zero};
            \begin{scope}[shift={(1.500,0.000)}]
                \pic (g2) at (0.500,0.500) {coge};
            \end{scope}
            \draw (g1-out-0) to[out=0,in=180] (g2-in-0);
            \begin{scope}[shift={(3.000,0.000)}]
                \pic[val=A] (g3) at (0.500,0.500) {comatrix};
            \end{scope}
            \draw (g2-out-0) to[out=0,in=180] (g3-in-0);
        \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{figure}
\end{proof}

Here the diagram expresses a cone with all its coordinates as outputs.
Bending the first $m$ wires back into inputs gives the required type $m\to n$.

\begin{lemma}[Completeness]
    If $[s]=[t]$ in $\mathbf{PCRel}$, then
    $s=t$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Bend the inputs into outputs and use the finitely generated normal
    form to write the two diagrams as $d_{V_s}$ and $d_{V_t}$.
    Their semantics are the conic hulls of the columns of $V_s$ and $V_t$.
    Since $[s]=[t]$, each column of $V_s$ is a non-negative combination
    of columns of $V_t$. Collecting the coefficients gives
    $V_s=V_tC$ for a non-negative matrix $C$.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:gpa-comparison} yields $d_{V_s}\subseteq d_{V_t}$.
    Reversing the roles of $s$ and $t$ yields the opposite inclusion,
    hence equality. Unbending the wires recovers $s=t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    $\mathbf{PCRel}$ is presented by
    $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}}$.
\end{theorem}

Together, soundness, expressivity, and completeness establish this
presentation~\cite[Corollary~21]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.


Cones always contain the origin, so they cannot represent an arbitrary
feasible region. We now reintroduce the affine generator $\rtop$ to
encode the right-hand side vector $b$ in $Ax\ge b$ and extend the theory
to general polyhedra.

We already have a language for cones, what is missing are the
constants. We add the affine point with its three equations from
Definition~\ref{def:affine-syntax}, and (P0), which tells us how it
interacts with the inequality generator. This gives the language of
polyhedra. We could also have started with Graphical Affine Algebra and added
the inequality generator with its axioms and (P0); either way, we
have the same generators and rules~\cite[Section~5]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.

\begin{definition}[Graphical Polyhedral Algebra]\label{def:gpa}
    We call $\mathbf{GPA}$ the SMIT obtained by adjoining the affine
    generator $\rtop:0\to1$, interpreted as the constant $1$, to
    $\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}$. We include the affine equations of Affine
    Algebra and the following additional axiom:
    \input{tikz/AxiomGPAAffine.tex}
\end{definition}

The inequality generator and its homogeneous axioms remain those of
Definition~\ref{def:gpa-conic}; only (P0) is new.
We write $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$ for the ordered PROP generated
by this extended theory, using the same construction as in
Definition~\ref{def:free-gpa-conic}.

\begin{theorem}[Normal Form]\label{thm:normalformgpa}
    For every diagram $c$ in $\mathbf{GPA}$, there exists a diagram $d$
    such that $c = d$ modulo the axioms and $d$ is of the form:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \input{tikz/NormalFormGPA.tex}
    \end{figure}
\end{theorem}


The affine block records the offsets, so the interpretation has the form
$Ax+b\ge By$. The normal form is established in
\cite[Theorem~24]{bonchi2021polyhedral}; its derivation is collected in
Appendix~\ref{appendix:gpa-proof-11}.

For completeness, we use the following two facts of the existing theory.
The first concerns non-empty relations; the second handles infeasibility.

\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous comparison]\label{lem:gpa-homogeneous-comparison}
    For non-empty polyhedra
    $P_i=\{z\in k^N\mid A_i z+b_i\ge0\}$, define
    \[
        P_i^H=\{(z,\lambda)\mid A_i z+b_i\lambda\ge0,\ \lambda\ge0\}.
    \]
    Then $P_1\subseteq P_2$ if and only if $P_1^H\subseteq P_2^H$.
    Replacing the affine constant in a normal-form diagram by a shared
    non-negative variable gives its homogeneous diagram. Derivable
    comparisons of those homogeneous diagrams give comparisons of the
    original diagrams by setting that variable to $1$.
\end{lemma}

These facts are established in
\cite[Lemma~25 and Theorem~26]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.
Their role is to let us use the complete conic calculus for non-empty
polyhedral relations.

\begin{lemma}[Empty normal form]\label{lem:gpa-empty-form}
    For each type $m\to n$, all diagrams of $\mathbf{GPA}$ whose
    interpretation is empty are equal to the same empty diagram
    $\bot_{m,n}$.
\end{lemma}

This fact is established in \cite[Theorem~27]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.
It is essential: the homogeneous comparison above assumes non-empty
polyhedra and cannot by itself cover infeasible constraints.


\begin{lemma}[Soundness]
    If $s=t$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$, then
    $[s]=[t]$ in $\mathbf{PolyRel}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    The homogeneous axioms are sound by the conic case, and the affine
    axioms inherited from $\mathbf{GAA}$ are already sound.
    The remaining axiom (P0) is valid because the constant $1$ satisfies
    $1\ge0$, so its scalar diagram has the same interpretation as the
    empty diagram. Thus all axioms preserve the polyhedral semantics.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Expressivity]
    For every $f:m\to n$ in $\mathbf{PolyRel}$ there exists a term
    $s:m\to n$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$ such that $[s]=f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    By Definition~\ref{polyform}, every polyhedron has the form
    $\{x \mid Ax \ge b\}$. Appealing to this representation directly,
    the polyhedron is expressed as the interpretation of the following
    diagram, where the generator $\rtop$ encodes the right-hand side
    vector $b$:
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \begin{tikzpicture}[axpic]
            \node[font=\Large] at (-0.100,0.500) {$\llbracket$};
            \pic (g1) at (0.500,0.500) {affine};
            \pic[val=b] (g2) at (1.500,0.500) {scalar};
            \draw (g1-out-0) to[out=0,in=180] (g2-in-0);
            \pic (g3) at (3.000,0.500) {coge};
            \draw (g2-out-0) to[out=0,in=180] (g3-in-0);
            \pic[val=A] (g4) at (5.000,0.500) {comatrix};
            \draw (g3-out-0) to[out=0,in=180] (g4-in-0);
            \node[font=\Large] at (6.300,0.500) {$\rrbracket$};
            \node[anchor=west] at (6.600,0.500)
                {$=\;\{x\in\mathbb{R}^N \mid Ax\ge b\}$};
        \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{figure}
    Indeed, the interpretation is a symmetric monoidal functor, so the
    diagram denotes the relational composite of the semantics of its three
    components:
    \[
        \llbracket \rtop\,;b\rrbracket=\{(\bullet,b)\},\qquad
        \llbracket \inlinegen{coge}\rrbracket=\{(u,w)\mid w\ge u\},\qquad
        \llbracket A^{\mathrm{op}}\rrbracket=\{(w,x)\mid w=Ax\}.
    \]
    Composing them eliminates the internal wires:
    \[
        \{(\bullet,x)\mid \exists\,w:\ w\ge b,\ w=Ax\}
        =\{x\in\mathbb{R}^N\mid Ax\ge b\},
    \]
    which is the claimed polyhedron. With $b=0$, this is the diagram of
    the conic case.
\end{proof}

As in the conic case, bending the first $m$ coordinates into inputs
gives a diagram with the required interface.

\begin{lemma}[Completeness]
    If $[s]=[t]$ in $\mathbf{PolyRel}$, then
    $s=t$ in $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    If the common interpretation is empty,
    Lemma~\ref{lem:gpa-empty-form} gives
    $s=\bot_{m,n}=t$.

    Otherwise, put both diagrams in affine normal form.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:gpa-homogeneous-comparison} gives equal homogeneous
    interpretations. Completeness of the conic fragment gives equality
    of the homogeneous diagrams. Setting the shared homogeneous
    variable to $1$ recovers equality of the affine normal forms,
    hence $s=t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    $\mathbf{PolyRel}$ is presented by $\mathrm{FreeP}_{\mathbf{GPA}}$.
\end{theorem}

This follows from soundness, expressivity, and completeness
\cite[Corollary~30]{bonchi2021polyhedral}.
Every polyhedral relation has a diagrammatic representative, and semantic
equality can be certified in $\mathbf{GPA}$. It is this presentation,
together with its normal form, that Chapter~\ref{sec:glp} builds on when
it adds the generator for the objective function.

\section{The transportation constraints revisited}\label{sec:gpa-travel}

Recall the divisible-traffic example from
Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}. We now draw its constraints in
Graphical Polyhedral Algebra, without the monetary objective. Every node
in the diagram below is a generator; nothing is abbreviated. We follow the
two-stage plan of Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}: the fuel budget is
split as $F=f+g$, with $f$ allocated to the first stage and $g$ to the
second, and the stages share the amount $q$ sent through $B$.

One small pattern occurs three times, so we name it first. The diagram
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[axpic]
    \pic (g1) at (0.500,0.000) {ge};
    \pic (g2) at (1.500,0.000) {cozero};
    \draw (g1-out-0) -- (g2-in-0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
denotes $\{(x,*)\mid x\ge0\}$: the inequality generator gives $x\ge y$ and
the white counit forces $y=0$. We call it the \emph{non-negativity test}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{tikz/TravelConstraints}}
    \caption{The transportation constraints in Graphical Polyhedral Algebra.
    The dotted boxes group the two stages $R_1:2\to1$ and $R_2:2\to0$;
    they are not new generators. The wires $f$, $g$, and $q$ are internal.}
    \label{fig:travel-constraints}
\end{figure}

We read the diagram from left to right.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Fuel split.} The white comultiplication on $F$ imposes
    $F=f+g$.
    \item \emph{First stage $R_1$.} The allowance $f$ passes through
    $\inlinegen{ge}$, which produces a value not exceeding $f$. The black
    node identifies this value with $p$, so $p\le f$. The white
    comultiplication then splits $p$ into the two roads leaving $A$,
    imposing $x_{AB}+x_{AC}=p$. Since this sum is the fuel used by the
    first stage, the black node states that this fuel fits in $f$.
    The black copy sends $x_{AB}$ both to the interface, as $q$, and to a
    non-negativity test; $x_{AC}$ goes to its own test.
    \item \emph{Second stage $R_2$.} The same pattern appears again. The
    allowance $g$ passes through $\inlinegen{ge}$ and the black node
    identifies the result with $q$. The common value is the flow
    $x_{BC}$ on the road $B\to C$, so $x_{BC}=q\le g$. A final
    non-negativity test gives $x_{BC}\ge0$.
\end{itemize}
Conservation at $B$ requires no node: the flow arriving at $B$ is the wire
$q$, and the flow leaving $B$ is the same wire.

Composing the generators inside each box gives the two stage relations
\[
\begin{aligned}
(p,f)\,R_1\,q &\iff \exists\,x_{AB},x_{AC}\ge0:\
x_{AB}+x_{AC}=p,\ x_{AB}=q,\ p\le f,\\
(q,g)\,R_2\,* &\iff \exists\,x_{BC}\ge0:\ x_{BC}=q,\ x_{BC}\le g.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the constraints of $U$ and $W$ in
Chapter~\ref{chap:lp-background}, with $g=F-f$. Relational composition
then eliminates the internal wires $f$, $g$, and $q$:
\[
(p,F)\ \text{accepted}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\,x_{AB},x_{BC},x_{AC}\ge0:\
x_{AB}+x_{AC}=p,\ x_{BC}=x_{AB},\ x_{AB}+x_{BC}+x_{AC}\le F.
\]
Indeed, a split $F=f+g$ with $p\le f$ and $x_{BC}\le g$ exists exactly
when $p+x_{BC}\le F$, and $p+x_{BC}=x_{AB}+x_{BC}+x_{AC}$.
These are precisely the original constraints. Every feasible pair
satisfies $p=x_{AB}+x_{AC}\ge0$ and $F\ge p+x_{BC}\ge p$; conversely,
when $0\le p\le F$, sending everything along the direct road is feasible.
The feasible pairs are therefore $p\ge0$ and $F\ge p$.

The diagram uses no affine point: every constraint is homogeneous, so it
lives in the conic fragment $\mathbf{GPA}_{\rtop}$, and its semantics is
the polyhedral cone $\{(p,F)\mid 0\le p\le F\}$. No cost is attached at
this stage: the diagram records feasibility only.
