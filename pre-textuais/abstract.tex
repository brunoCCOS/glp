\begin{center}
  \textbf{\MakeUppercase{Abstract}}
\end{center}

\noindent
This dissertation develops a categorical approach to graphical algebra and a
compositional calculus for optimization problems. It investigates the
relationship between composition, variable elimination, and optimization,
showing how optimization arises from the composition of quantale-valued
relations in concrete settings. The central result is the presentation of a
complete monoidal theory for right-hand side parametric linear programming.

The development begins with quantales and relations and their connection to
metric spaces through the Lawvere quantale. It then recalls Graphical
Polyhedral Algebra (\textbf{GPA}), the existing diagrammatic algebra for
polyhedra, and presents its soundness and completeness proofs. Finally, it
extends this theory with a single generator and three axioms, moving from a
language for polyhedral relations to one that denotes parametric linear
programs.

The resulting calculus contributes to the broader goal of providing an
intuitive language for mathematical and computational reasoning in
optimization. String diagrams encode structural bookkeeping in a compact
graphical syntax, making the underlying operations and their interactions
accessible to manipulation and reasoning, particularly in complex systems.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent
Keywords: category theory; string diagrams; linear programming; convex
optimization; symmetric monoidal categories; weighted relations; quantales;
graphical algebra; normal forms; completeness.
